Paralinguistic features, such as pitch, stress, timing, filler words, hedges, and voice quality, can alter how an utterance is pragmatically preceived.

These cues might assist listeners to derive the correct implicatures. In accordance with Grice, paralinguistic cues can serve as supplement upon detecting if a speaker is deliberately flouting a conversational maxim and conveying an implicature.

Audio-enabled language models can process speech directly, without the effort of transcribing the speech into text first. But it remains unclear whether their pragmatic judgmnets are genuinely sensitive to paralinguistic contrasts or reflect model-specific response preferences.
