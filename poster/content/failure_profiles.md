### Kimi-Audio: `literal + none`

Kimi-Audio produced internally consistent judgments but largely
failed to change its interpretation in response to marked delivery.
Its high neutral accuracy reflected a strong literal/none preference.

### Qwen2-Audio: `implicature + quality`

Qwen2-Audio produced highly consistent but condition-invariant
responses. Its perfect performance on marked Quality items was
explained by a strong preference for the `quality` label rather than
maxim-specific discrimination.

### Step-Audio 2: `implicature + none`

Step-Audio 2 often detected an implicature-like interpretation without
mapping it onto Quality, Quantity, or Manner. Reading–maxim consistency
fell to 31.1% in neutral audio and 24.4% in marked audio.

These patterns suggest that detecting an implicature-like
interpretation and identifying the relevant Gricean maxim are separable capabilities.
